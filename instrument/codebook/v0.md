# Codebook v0: mental operations in expert physics problem solving

Status: draft, not frozen. Definitions, Include and Exclude are filled; examples marked (SIM) come from simulated sessions (Sonnet role-play) and are replaced by pilot examples. The version used for Stage A is frozen before the first data session.

## What is coded

A **mental operation**: a cue, decision, representation choice, check or heuristic that a novice could be taught. The coder writes each operation once as a statement, "in situation S, do A (because C)", with an `op_id` and the units it was found in. The statement is what cross-expert matching and trace corroboration compare.

Not operations, so not coded: restating the givens; arithmetic and algebra steps; a bare result ("0.8 metres"); filler, hesitation and social talk. A student error is coded only for the operation it implies, when the unit states the correct action or why the error is wrong; advice to students ("I always tell them: sketch the pendulum") is coded as the expert's own operation.

## Units

- **Trace unit:** one think-aloud segment, a row of `trace_units.csv` (`segment_id` such as `A1-s002`) as corrected in trace review. The coder reads the neighbouring segments of the same problem and the written-work snapshot for context, but codes only what the segment itself says or, with the snapshot, shows being done. An operation that runs across segments is coded on every segment that states part of it.
- **Probe unit:** one sentence of an expert probe answer, a row of `coder_units.csv` (`unit_id`). The export splits answers at `.`, `!` or `?`; a unit is never re-split or merged by the coder.
- The written work is context for trace units, not a unit of its own.

## Cardinality and agreement

A unit may carry zero, one or several operation types. Each type is decided **present or absent** for every unit, independently of the others: a unit that fits two definitions gets both codes. Every unit gets a row, including units with no operation, with a present/absent value for each type. Agreement is computed per code (present/absent over all units), with Krippendorff's α per code, separately for trace and probe units, and the target of 0.70 on each; a code below target is revised or merged before single coding. A type present in under 5% of the double-coded units cannot give a stable α: it is reported by count, and the pilots decide whether it is merged.

## Type (map §9 Phase 1 taxonomy)

| Type | Definition | Include | Exclude | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Omitted prerequisite | Knowledge from outside the principles of this topic that the expert uses as a step | A mathematical, geometric or earlier-physics relation or skill used in a step: proportional scaling, trigonometry of the set-up, reading a graph. Code the use; whether ordinary material states it is decided later against the baseline, never by the coder | The topic's own principles and when they apply (→ Decision criterion); the physical picture (→ Conceptual model); plain arithmetic | (SIM) "Height scales with speed squared, so four ninths of 1.8"; "cos theta is one minus h over L" |
| Perceptual cue | A feature of this problem that the expert notices and that triggers a decision | A word, quantity, condition or feature of the statement, diagram or situation that the expert says they noticed in this problem, together with what it led to: "stick", "embeds", "frictionless", "starts at rest". A condition that appears only in a counterfactual is not a cue (→ Decision criterion) | A feature of the expert's own result (→ Error-checking routine); the general rule with no feature of this problem named (→ the code of what it decides: Decision criterion, Representation choice, Error-checking routine or Metacognitive judgment) | (SIM) "Bullet embeds, so again a sticking collision" |
| Representation choice | Choosing a diagram, system, coordinate, reference level, or quantity or form to work in | Choosing the system (block plus bullet), the zero of height, symbolic versus numeric working, a ratio or intermediate quantity to carry, a sign convention; the reason for the choice when given | The order of stages or intermediate results (→ Decomposition strategy); choosing between principles, methods or a recalled result (→ Decision criterion) | (SIM) "I like pausing on that ratio though, because it's a clean, portable number" |
| Decomposition strategy | Splitting the problem into stages or sub-problems, and where to cut | Naming the stages, their order or their boundary: "first the collision, then the swing"; treating a part as a separate problem | Which principle applies within a stage (→ Decision criterion: when a unit states both, code both) | (SIM) "Then the pair compresses the spring": the second of two stages |
| Decision criterion | The condition that decides which principle, equation, method or recalled result applies | A condition-to-method rule, stated for this problem or in general: "they stick, so momentum, not energy"; "elastic, so both conserved"; "frictionless, so mechanical energy conserved"; a counterfactual that changes the method ("if it weren't frictionless I'd need work against friction") | Noticing the feature alone (→ Perceptual cue: when a unit names the feature and the rule, code both); a reason for a representation (→ Representation choice) | (SIM) "Then they stick, so that part isn't energy, it's momentum" |
| Conceptual model | The physical picture the expert reasons with | A mechanism or causal account of what happens physically: where energy goes in a sticking collision, what happens during the impact versus after, why a light target leaves faster | A formula recalled with no physical account: Omitted prerequisite if it comes from outside this topic, otherwise not coded; a condition-to-method rule (→ Decision criterion) | (SIM) "The collision itself already threw away most of the kinetic energy as heat and sound" |
| Error-checking routine | A check on a step or result, or a guard against a known error | A check whose basis is stated: comparison with a bound or expectation, limiting case, order of magnitude, units, sign, redoing a step, clean-number plausibility; a named trap the expert avoids at a step ("I shouldn't use the initial kinetic energy, that's the classic trap") | "That makes sense" or "good" with no basis (→ Metacognitive judgment); a student error that states neither a correct action nor a reason (not coded); the comparison that answers the question asked ("more than 0.5, so yes it gets over") | (SIM) "That's lower, which makes sense": lower than the starting height after a sticking collision |
| Metacognitive judgment | Monitoring of progress, difficulty, confidence or plan | Judging a step routine, hard, uncertain or finished; deciding to slow down, redo or stop; deciding when a check is needed ("if this were wrong, would I even notice?"); an unexplained "that makes sense". Code Error-checking routine too when the check and its basis are stated | A check with a stated basis alone (→ Error-checking routine) | (SIM) "That's actually a weak spot in my compressed work" |
| Disciplinary norm / epistemic standard | What counts as an acceptable answer or argument | Standards of evidence, precision or form: "a sanity check, not a proof"; appropriate significant figures; stating assumptions; preferring a general symbolic result | A particular check carried out (→ Error-checking routine: when a unit states the standard and the check, code both); a personal preference with no standard (→ Representation choice) | (SIM) "It's a quick sanity check, not a proof" |

## Source (assigned from the key, never by the coder)

## Status

| Status | Rule |
|---|---|
| trace-only | Extracted from the think-aloud and written work by a coder who has not seen probe material |
| probe-added, performed | Named first in a probe answer, then corroborated in the trace by a third coder blind to interviewer, with decoys mixed in |
| reported only | Named in a probe answer, not corroborated in the trace |
| contradicted | The trace shows the expert doing something incompatible at the same step; absence from the trace is reported only |

A hypothetical ("I'd want to also verify…") is reported only unless the trace shows it done.

## Agreement

Each coder returns `coder_units.csv` with a `label` column added, one row per unit, holding the unit's codes spelled exactly as in the Type table:
- several codes are separated by `;` (`Perceptual cue; Decision criterion`);
- `none` marks a unit coded as containing no operation;
- a blank cell means the unit was not coded, and it is left out of agreement.

A unit ID may appear only once per file. A code not in the Type table is refused (case is ignored). Both commands print how many units both coders coded and how many each left blank or missing, and refuse fewer than 2 shared units. Agreement is computed by `probe-code alpha A.csv B.csv --multi`, which gives Krippendorff's α with MASI distance (Passonneau 2006), and by `probe-code kappa A.csv B.csv --multi`, which gives Cohen's κ for each code over the units both coders coded, scored as present or absent. A code that is constant for both coders has no κ (`n/a`). Without `--multi`, both commands read one label per unit, as for K0's error categories.

## Cross-expert matching

Two operations are **the same** if a novice taught either would perform the same action in the same situation. Record matches in `matches.csv` (`op_id_a, op_id_b, same`); agreement by κ.

## Framework tag

Tag `published=yes` if the operation's statement appears in one of these frameworks; the tag never changes a code:
- Heller & Reif (1984): a prescribed procedure for describing the problem in physics terms before solving it;
- Dufresne et al. (1992): principle-first hierarchical analysis, naming the principle, then its justification, then the procedure;
- Docktor et al. (2015): the Conceptual Problem Solving strategy, identifying the principle, justifying it and planning before writing equations.
