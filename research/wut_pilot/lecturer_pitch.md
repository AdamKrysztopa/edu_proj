# Turning what you know into material your students can use

*A one-page invitation for a lecturer at Politechnika Warszawska. Course: **[PLACEHOLDER: course name, code, semester]**.*

## The problem

Every course has steps an experienced lecturer does without thinking: the check you make before choosing a method, the warning sign that tells you a result is wrong. These steps are rarely in the slides or the textbook, because to an expert they are obvious. They are often exactly where students get stuck.

## What the tool does

1. **Reads what is written.** Your course materials (slides, notes, problem sets, the syllabus) and the textbook chapters you name.
2. **Maps where the written material is thin.** It produces a gap map: the topics where the materials state a result but not how to get there, or where a decision step is missing.
3. **Asks you only those questions.** No general interview about the whole course; only questions about the gaps.
4. **Turns your answers into learning material.** Short worked examples, hints or checklists, written from your answers and reviewed by you before any student sees them.
5. **Shows where students still struggle.** Students who volunteer try short exercises; we report back, for the group as a whole, where difficulty remains.

## What we would ask of you

- **Materials:** access to the course materials you choose to share. You decide what is in and what is out.
- **One interview of 60–90 minutes**, in person or online, or answered in writing if you prefer. Audio recording only with your separate consent. **[PLACEHOLDER: confirm length after the demonstrator.]**
- **About 2 hours** to review the gap map and the learning material before students see it.
- **About 30 minutes** to let us present the study to your students, or to forward a short announcement.
- **In total: about 4–5 hours over [PLACEHOLDER: N] weeks.**

## What your students would do

Taking part is voluntary and has no effect on grades. Volunteers would spend about **[PLACEHOLDER: 60] minutes** in total, outside graded work: using the learning material, trying **[PLACEHOLDER: 2–3]** short ungraded exercises and answering a 5-minute questionnaire. You would not be told who took part. Their answers are stored under a code, not a name.

## What you get back

- **A gap map of your course:** where the written materials are thin, topic by topic. It is private to you unless you choose to share it.
- **Learning material built from your answers**, yours to reuse in the course.
- **A group-level report** of where students still struggle after using it. It never shows an individual student, and small groups are not reported separately.

## Risks and how we handle them

| Risk | How it is handled |
|---|---|
| Students feel pressure because their lecturer is involved | Recruitment and consent are handled by the research team, not by you; you do not learn who took part; nothing affects grades; the group report reaches you only after final grades are submitted. |
| AI-written material contains errors | You review and approve every item before students see it. |
| The gap map reads as a critique of your course | It describes the written materials, not your teaching, and stays private to you. |
| Student data | Pseudonymised, stored at PW, reported at group level only, handled under GDPR; nothing starts without a positive opinion from PW's research ethics team. |
| Course text sent to AI models from outside companies | Disclosed in the consent forms; no student names or identifiers are sent; providers listed in advance **[PLACEHOLDER: provider list]**. |
| Your time | Fixed in advance as above; you can stop at any point. |

**If your course covers conservation laws in physics:** anyone who sees the material we build cannot later take part, as an expert or interviewer, in a separate physics study we have planned. We would tell you which roles this affects before you decide.
