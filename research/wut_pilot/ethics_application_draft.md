## II. Project description (form section II)

### 1. Rationale and aims

Course materials omit steps experts find obvious, often where students struggle. The tool (1) reads the course materials, (2) maps where they are thin, (3) asks the lecturer only about those areas, (4) turns the answers into learning material, and (5) measures, at group level, where students still struggle. Aims (exploratory): whether targeted questions draw out content absent from the materials, and whether remaining difficulty concentrates where the materials were thin. [PLACEHOLDER: bibliography]

### 2. Procedure and methodology

- **Duration and place:** [PLACEHOLDER: dates], within [PLACEHOLDER: course name, code, faculty, semester].
- **Lecturer:** shares chosen materials; one 60–90-minute interview (or written answers), recorded only with separate consent; about 2 hours of review.
- **Students:** use the reviewed learning material and complete [PLACEHOLDER: 2–3] short ungraded exercises and a 5-minute questionnaire, about [PLACEHOLDER: 60] minutes in all, online, outside graded coursework.
- **Methods:** non-invasive; no deception. Answers are scored by rubric with AI assistance, checked by a human, and aggregated by topic for the group.

### 3. Sample and recruitment

- **Students:** adults enrolled in the host course, target [PLACEHOLDER: n]. Excluded: under-18s.

- **Lecturer–student power relation:** the lecturer does not learn who took part; participation and withdrawal have no effect on grades; no course credit for taking part; the group report reaches the lecturer only after final grades are submitted; groups below [PLACEHOLDER: 10] students are not reported.

### 8. Other ethically relevant aspects: AI

- **EU AI Act.** Annex III point 3(b) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 lists as high-risk "AI systems intended to be used to evaluate learning outcomes, including when those outcomes are used to steer the learning process of natural persons in educational and vocational training institutions at all levels" (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj). Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 amends Art. 113, third paragraph, point (c), so that Chapter III, Sections 1, 2 and 3 (except Art. 6(5)) apply from 2 December 2027 to AI systems classified as high-risk under Art. 6(2) and Annex III (point (c)(i)) (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj). An Annex III listing is subject to the Art. 6(3) exemption: such a system is not high-risk where it does not pose a significant risk of harm to health, safety or fundamental rights, including by not materially influencing the outcome of decision making, and one of the conditions in Art. 6(3) is met; a system that profiles natural persons is always high-risk. Art. 2(6) excludes systems developed and put into service for the sole purpose of scientific research and development, and Art. 2(8) excludes research before placing on the market or putting into service, but not testing in real-world conditions. Whether either applies is for PW's legal counsel. Regardless, no individual student evaluation is produced, shown or used for any decision.
- **Automated decisions:** none about any person (Art. 22 GDPR).
- **AI errors:** human review of all AI scoring and all AI-written material.
