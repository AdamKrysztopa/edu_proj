# Review D — Clarity and Reproducibility (Round 2)

Reviewer D worked independently from `draft2.pdf` (143 pp.) and `report/{main.tex,sections/*.tex,bibliography.bib,figures/src}`. Did not read other `review_*.md`/`revision_*.md` files.

## Summary

The report is unusually disciplined for its length: sections consistently open by naming the question they answer and close with a forward pointer or a `findingbox` (checked 01, 02, 03, 04, 12, 13, 14, 17 — all pass). Citation sampling (25 keys, Crossref-checked) and reproducibility testing (all four `pytest` suites, the offline gap-map replay) found **no critical defects** — see items 20–21. The main defects are: (a) sentence length concentrated in a few sections, (b) a real terminology clash the project's own drafting rules explicitly forbid, (c) figure text printing below 7 pt almost everywhere because figures are drawn wider than the text block and then scaled down, and (d) American/British spelling drift in exactly the words the style guide calls out.

## 3. Terminology consistency

**CRITICAL — H3 name collision, against the project's own binding rule.** `00_outline.md` states explicitly: *"The pivot hypothesis in 11_programme.md ('H3: targeted interviewing without reconstruction') must be renamed in prose ... so it does not clash with H3"* (H3 = "prioritized questions cut expert time," per the same document and `02_question.tex`'s hypothesis table). The report does rename the pivot to "the interview-only pivot" almost everywhere — but at **`22_roadmap.tex:56`** and **`23_workpackages.tex:143`** it then re-parenthesizes it as *"the interview-only pivot (H3 tested without the reconstruction prior)"* — reintroducing exactly the forbidden clash. A reader who has just learned H3 = "prioritized questions cut expert time" (Section 2) will misread this as the same H3. Fix: drop the parenthetical "(H3 ...)" at both locations, or rename it "(the pivot hypothesis, tested without the reconstruction prior)."

**MINOR — spelling drift: judgment/judgement.** Project style (`00_outline.md`) mandates American spelling throughout prose. Count across `report/sections/*.tex`: **34** instances of American "judgment/judgments" vs. **10** of British "judgement/judgements" — the British spellings are in running prose, not inside `\code{}` (where British spelling is permitted for code identifiers), e.g. `06_architecture.tex:136` ("Judgements are cached to..."), `18_platform.tex:294` ("closure judgement"), `A_claims.tex:21,79,127,135`. Fix: search-and-replace "judgement"→"judgment" outside `\code{}`/`\repo{}` spans.

**MINOR — spelling drift: licence/license.** American "license" used 11 times; British "licence" used 3 times, all in `24_team.tex:72-73` ("Data-protection / licence advisor," "Dataset licence checks") and `25_resources.tex:148` ("author contact, licences"). Fix to "license(s)".

**MINOR — spelling: "mislabelled."** `D_defects.tex:65` ("Report mislabelled verifier-rejected claims...") — British doubling of the "l"; American is "mislabeled" (the report correctly uses "labeled" elsewhere, e.g. `05_scope.tex:11`, so this is an isolated slip).

**MINOR — acronym SESOI never expanded in body text.** "Smallest effect of interest" is spelled out in full at `02_question.tex:37,50` and `23_workpackages.tex:34`, but the acronym **SESOI** itself first appears at `22_roadmap.tex:78` and `23_workpackages.tex:289` with no inline tie to its expansion — a reader only learns "SESOI = Smallest Effect Size Of Interest" from the Appendix F glossary (`F_glossary.tex:140`), which many readers will not reach before hitting the acronym in Section 22–23. Fix: at first acronym use (`02_question.tex` or wherever SESOI is first typed), write "the smallest effect of interest (SESOI)."

**Checked and consistent (no issues found):** World A/B/C usage; "gap candidate" vs. "retrieval gap" (never conflated); "absence check (closure)" convention; "gap map" capitalization (87 lowercase vs. 4 sentence-initial capital "Gap map" — acceptable, not a defect); H1/H2/H3 definitions in Section 2 match `00_outline.md`'s ruling; V0–V8, S0–S6, MS1–MS8, WP1–WP11 numbering is consistent everywhere sampled (Table 19, Table 24, Table 25, Figures 11/14/15 all agree).

## 4. Figures

**MAJOR — systematic sub-7pt text from scaling, not just design choice.** Checked every figure's `\includegraphics` width against its native PDF page size (computed from `pdfinfo`) and its smallest declared TikZ `\fontsize{}` in `figures/src/*.tex`. Text block width is 425.2 pt (a4paper, `left=28mm,right=32mm`). Because most figures are drawn wider than the text block and then placed at 85–92% of `\linewidth`, their nominal 6.5–7 pt minimum font prints *smaller* than 7 pt:

| fig15_gantt (Fig 15, WP/Gantt) | 6.5 pt | 0.92 li | 430.3 | **~5.9 pt** |
| fig12_residual (Fig 10) | 7 pt | 0.85 li | 415.1 | **~6.1 pt** |
| fig08_closure (Fig 7) | 6.6 pt | 0.92 li | 387.3 | ~6.7 pt |

| fig03_lenses (Fig 2) | 7 pt | 0.85 li | 424.5 | ~6.0 pt |
| fig04_architecture (Fig 4) | 7 pt | 0.92 li | 420.9 | ~6.5 pt |
| fig11_platform (Fig 13) | 7 pt | 0.92 li | 411.6 | ~6.6 pt |

No figure was referenced in text after it first appeared (checked all 15 — each is introduced by name before or at the point of inclusion). ## 6. Reproducibility
